What if a small living world could tell us what it needs?

Sirona is an environment-control project. Its goal is to connect sensing, interpretation and control of the conditions in which plants and other organisms live. It has two specialised branches: Tera for terrariums and Aqua for aquariums.

A shared principle, different needs. Tera focuses on terrarium environments; Aqua focuses on aquarium environments. We start by understanding each environment, then progress through measurement towards verified control. Autonomy is the long-term direction.

TERA / UNDERSTANDING THE ENVIRONMENTEDUCATIONAL AIR MODEL
FROM READINGS TO RELATIONSHIPS

Nature has
a signal.

We want to learn to read it.

Tera is Sirona's terrarium environment-control branch. It is intended to connect microclimate monitoring with lighting, fogging and ventilation control. The first terrarium gives us a way to observe how a living plant responds to changes.

One useful variable is air VPD: the difference between saturation vapour pressure and actual vapour pressure. It combines air temperature and relative humidity to describe the air's drying potential.

TERRARIUM↔SENSORS↔SIRONA TERA

Planned backbone: ESP32 + sensors → MQTT → dashboard and history. A working hardware-to-dashboard connection for the first pilot has not yet been verified.

SIRONA BELOW THE SURFACE

Balance
below the surface.

A different world. Its own requirements.

Aqua is Sirona's aquarium environment-control branch. Its goal is to connect monitoring of the aquatic environment with lighting, filtration and water-circulation control.

Life underwater needs its own measurements, relationships and control strategies. Sirona is the shared system concept; Aqua adapts it to aquarium environments.

BEYOND ONE GLASS ENCLOSURE

One Sirona.
Different environments.

Sirona is the shared environment-control system concept. Tera and Aqua specialise it for terrariums and aquariums. The long-term vision is modular expansion into other environments, using a shared data language and rules suited to each living world.

A research direction for Sirona. Expansion will depend on lessons from the first pilot.

A GOOD DISCUSSION STARTS WITH A QUESTION

What can we learn
from the living world?

We are building, observing and sharing what we learn. Do you grow Nepenthes, keep an aquarium or connect living environments with electronics? We would value your practical feedback.

THREE QUESTIONS WE WOULD LIKE TO TEST
  1. Where would you place temperature and humidity sensors to avoid direct fog and misleading readings?
  2. What failure should the first controller detect before it is allowed to switch equipment?
  3. Which trend or warning would make this useful in your terrarium or aquarium?

Current stage: manual terrarium pilot plus a static project website. AI-generated images show the intended appearance. Hardware telemetry and autonomous control are not yet verified.
